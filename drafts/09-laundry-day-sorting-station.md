# A Laundry-Day Sorting Station Using Baskets for Lights, Darks and Delicates

**Status: DRAFT — NOT APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION**

Laundry can feel like it takes over the house when clothes, towels and bedding are all waiting in one pile. A simple sorting station makes the job easier because you decide where each item goes before wash day, instead of sorting everything at the last minute.

You don't need a dedicated laundry room. A corner of a bathroom, utility area or wardrobe can work, as long as the baskets are easy to reach and the area stays dry and ventilated.

## Start with three practical categories

For many households, three baskets are enough:

- **Lights:** whites, pale colours and fabrics that can be washed together according to their care labels
- **Darks:** darker clothing and textiles that may transfer colour
- **Delicates:** items that need gentler handling or a separate wash

If you have a large family or frequently wash towels and bedding, you may add a fourth basket for bulky items. Keep categories broad so the system is easy to maintain.

## Choose baskets that suit your space

Look at the available floor space before buying baskets. Tall baskets work well in narrow corners, while shorter, wider baskets may be easier for children or other family members to use. If you have a small utility area, a compact set that fits under a counter may be more practical.

Baskets with handles are easier to carry, and breathable designs help prevent laundry from sitting in a closed, damp space. Avoid placing laundry baskets where they block a walkway or create a tripping hazard.

If you use baskets around the home for other tasks too, the [linen-closet basket system](https://homesnbeyond.in/blogs/blogs/the-linen-closet-edit-assigning-a-basket-to-every-household-textile) shares ideas for assigning a place to each household textile.

## Make sorting part of everyone's routine

Place the baskets where people naturally change clothes. If they are tucked away behind a door, family members may continue leaving laundry on the floor. A clear label or simple colour cue can make it obvious where an item belongs.

For children, use low baskets and keep the categories simple. You can start with one basket for everyday clothes and introduce sorting gradually as they get older.

## Keep delicates from becoming a mixed pile

A small mesh bag or separate container can hold items that need extra attention, such as pieces with delicate trims or care instructions. Before washing, check labels, close zippers, turn items as instructed and separate anything that could snag other fabrics.

Don't rely on the basket label alone to determine a wash cycle. The care label on each item still matters.

## Add a small folding and return zone

If you have room, keep a clean surface for folding and a basket for items that need to go back to bedrooms. This helps prevent clean laundry from becoming another pile.

You can also use a separate basket for clean towels or bedding waiting to be put away. For households that manage baby laundry separately, a small dedicated basket can make frequent changes easier. The [first-month nursery textile checklist](https://homesnbeyond.in/blogs/blogs/the-first-month-at-home-a-calm-practical-nursery-textile-checklist) includes a few ideas for keeping baby textiles organised without overcomplicating the routine.

## Make the system easy to reset

Once a week, empty the baskets, wipe the area if needed and put them back in their designated spots. If one category constantly overflows, adjust the basket size or your wash schedule rather than adding more containers immediately.

A sorting station works best when it is uncomplicated. A few well-placed baskets can turn laundry from a household-wide pile into a routine that everyone can follow.
