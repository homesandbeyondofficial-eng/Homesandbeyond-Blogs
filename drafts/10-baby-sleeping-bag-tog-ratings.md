# Baby Sleeping Bag TOG Ratings: A Parent-Friendly Guide to the Label

**Status: DRAFT — NOT APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION**

If you've been shopping for a baby sleeping bag, you may have noticed a TOG rating on the label. It is a measure of thermal resistance, often used to help compare how insulating a sleep product is. The number can be useful, but it isn't a stand-alone instruction for dressing your baby.

A sleeping bag's TOG rating, your room temperature, the baby's clothing layers and the manufacturer's guidance all need to be considered together.

## What does TOG mean?

TOG is a unit used to describe thermal insulation. In simple terms, a higher TOG rating generally means a product provides more insulation than a lower-rated one, when comparing products under the same standard.

It doesn't mean that a particular TOG rating is right for every baby or every room. Two products may also differ in fabric, fit and design, so always read the instructions for the specific sleeping bag you are using.

## Why room temperature matters

The same sleeping bag can feel different in a cool room and in a warm room. A baby's sleepwear and the room's temperature are part of the overall warmth, so avoid treating the TOG number as a universal dressing formula.

If you use a sleeping bag, follow the manufacturer's room-temperature guide and layer chart. Use a reliable room thermometer if you're unsure how warm the room is, and check your baby's comfort in the way recommended by your healthcare professional. Avoid relying on hands and feet alone as they can feel cooler than the baby's body.

## Read the whole label, not only the TOG number

Before using a sleeping bag, check:
- The recommended temperature range and layering chart
- The correct size for your baby's height and weight, as stated by the manufacturer
- The neck and arm openings, so the bag fits as intended and your baby cannot slip down inside
- Whether the design has sleeves, removable layers or other features, and how those features should be used
- Washing and care instructions

Do not use a sleeping bag that is too large or has been altered in a way that affects its fit. Avoid adding loose blankets over or around the baby as a way to adjust warmth unless you are following specific safe-sleep guidance from a qualified source.

## Sleeping bag or blanket?

A wearable sleeping bag is designed to be worn, while a blanket is a separate layer. Their fit and use are different. If you are comparing what to buy for your baby, our [sleepwear and sleeping-bag layering guide](https://homesnbeyond.in/blogs/blogs/baby-sleepwear-and-sleeping-bags-understanding-the-difference-between-layers) explains the difference between the two.

For newborns and young babies, follow current safe-sleep guidance from your paediatrician or a recognised health authority. Keep the sleep space free of loose bedding, pillows, quilts and soft toys, and use a firm, flat sleep surface with a correctly fitted sheet. A sleeping bag should never be treated as a reason to add extra bedding.

## Check your baby's comfort safely

If your baby seems hot, sweaty, flushed or unusually uncomfortable, review the layers and room conditions promptly and follow medical guidance if you're concerned. If your baby is unwell or you are unsure how to dress them for sleep, ask your healthcare professional for advice rather than relying on a chart alone.

## Use TOG as a guide, not a shortcut

TOG ratings can help you understand and compare sleeping bags, but they work best alongside the manufacturer's instructions, the room temperature and your baby's clothing. Choose a product that fits properly, keep the sleep environment safe and adjust layers thoughtfully.

When it comes to baby sleep, a clear label is helpful, but safe setup and careful use matter just as much.
